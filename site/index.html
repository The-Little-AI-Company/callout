Two lanes, one keypress

Fast lane, under a second

What the text's own shape says: pushes urgency, big numbers with no source, contradicts itself, attacks people instead of the point. Plus how it's trying to persuade you: fear, false dilemmas, unnamed experts, fake countdowns, and thirty other techniques, each with the sentence that shows it.

Header on every result: Signals in the text itself. Not a truth check.

Deep lane, a few seconds

Each specific claim gets a traffic light and a one-line why. Green needs a fetched source that supports it and none that contradict. Red needs a contradicting source or a quote that isn't in the source it cites. Gray means unsure, and it says what was missing.

Every verdict comes with the passage and the link. No source, no verdict.

The honesty promise

  • No verdict without a shown source. No "false" without a contradicting source or a fabricated quote.
  • Unsure is a first-class answer, and every unsure names its reason.
  • The AI helper never judges. It extracts, transcribes, and writes summaries, and every sentence it writes is checked against the sources before you see it.
  • Confidence in words, never numbers. Jokes in the loading screen, never in a verdict.

Your keys, your machine

Callout has no server and no account. You add three API keys once: TypeSafe for judgment (required), Tavily for web search, and an LLM provider for the helper (DeepSeek by default). Keys sit in the Windows credential store. Text you check goes only to those services and to the pages Callout fetches for evidence. It never reaches us.
